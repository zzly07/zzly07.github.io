00 / OVERVIEWSIDE A

Overview

Wide Asleep is a first-person psychological horror game about a teenage girl who loses her senses inside a dream. Across levels, she recovers them and uses them to solve puzzles and survive stealth encounters.

I designed the core Senses gameplay and player experience from the ground up, then shaped a Hearing-focused demo level around it. My work also covered enemy behavior, interactions, UI, and sensory feedback. The demo takes place in an imagined psychiatric ward, intended as the second level after the player regains Hearing.

My contribution

  • Designed the core gameplay and player experience from the ground up, using temporary Senses to turn sensory deprivation into decisions for stealth and puzzles.
  • Defined what Vision and Hearing reveal, their mutually exclusive use, and their cooldown to balance uncertainty with readable choices.
  • Designed and iterated the Hearing-focused demo level, from safe onboarding and branching routes to the climax encounter and the Puzzle.
  • Designed enemy patrol and detection behavior, then prototyped and implemented the Senses and interaction systems needed to test the experience.
01 / DESIGN NOTES↘

Senses: designing perception

The Goal

The starting design question was how to express the protagonist's distress through sensory deprivation without leaving players overly confused and frustrated.

Solution: Active Perception Abilities

I built the core loop around temporary Vision and Hearing abilities: they cannot be active together, and each use is followed by a cooldown. The choice of which sense to use, and when, becomes part of both stealth and puzzle solving.

Vision reveals hiding spots and throwable objects. Hearing reveals enemies, noisy surfaces, and objective notes through walls. Each ability supplies different, actionable information; during the cooldown, players must rely on what they remember.

Implementation: Xray shader, Post-Processing

To make those rules readable in play, I prototyped Vision with raycasts and object outlines, and Hearing with a depth-tested X-ray shader. Changes to post-processing and audio signal the switch between deprivation and an active Sense. I also implemented puzzle, pickup, and distraction interactions.

02 / DESIGN NOTES↘

Hearing level: flow and pacing

The demo is set in an imagined psychiatric ward, intended as the second level after the player regains Hearing. Its layout teaches players to use Hearing to locate objective notes and avoid enemies.

Safe Zones — Two enemy-free opening areas let players experiment with Hearing. Spotlights guide them toward the first note and introduce the hear, locate, collect loop.

Nonlinear Exploration — The connected central area branches into rooms containing more notes. Darkness encourages players to detect patrols through walls before moving.

Climax — Tight corners and overlapping patrol routes make the final note the most intense stealth encounter.

Resolution — After collecting all five notes, players assemble a jigsaw puzzle that reveals a musical sequence to play. A one-way shortcut lets them return for missed notes without crossing the highest-danger zone again.

03 / DESIGN NOTES↘

Iteration and Reflection

I designed enemy behaviors and tuned detection ranges and patrol routes with the aim of creating stressful but fair encounters. Lighting guides navigation while preserving the horror atmosphere.

We iterated on the level design based on playtest feedback to create a more thrilling and engaging experience. During the iteration, I focused on adjusting the proportion and placement of safe or advantageous areas and dangerous or disadvantageous areas to create a fierce yet fair player experience.
